Autosweep Companion App — UX Audit

A heuristic review of the current Autosweep RFID mobile app (iOS, v4.0.0.1) for San Miguel Infrastructure's 1M+ toll customers, based on 41 captured screens covering 17 distinct surfaces, including the full reload flow.

Executive summary

The Autosweep app is structurally a thin wrapper around a small set of back-office functions — look up an account, generate a statement, hand off to a payment provider, send a support message. It is not yet built around the questions a toll customer actually has on the road: Do I have enough load to get through the next gantry? Did that charge look right? How do I top up in the next 30 seconds?

The single most consequential finding is that the account balance — the one number every user opens the app for — is not shown on Home. It sits one tap deep on a screen titled "Account," beneath the same visual weight as a "Delete Account" button. Two of the four primary navigation tabs are effectively non-functional: Toll Calculator is disabled (it opens a "Feature Disabled" alert), and Traffic Updates is an embedded corporate website that loads blank, then shows a spinner, then blocks the map with a 2018 privacy-consent modal. A third destination in the Menu, Announcement, renders empty.

The app also has no visible transaction history. The only route to "what was I charged?" is a Statement of Account generator that requires six dropdown selections and whose output is never shown in-app. Trust-critical flows — reload, dispute a charge, report an unreadable sticker — exist but are buried, jargon-laden, or hand off to a third party without confirmation.

The reload flow itself (screens R1–R10) is more complete than the method picker suggests: it names real providers (BPI, RCBC, UnionBank, GCash, GrabPay, Maya), shows a clear payment summary and discloses the fee before charging. But it introduces its own trust problems — bank credentials are entered inside an Autosweep-branded webview, a ₱13 convenience fee (6.5% on a ₱200 load) is revealed only at the final summary, full card details are typed into what looks like a native form, and the GCash handoff renders a blank QR panel with no captured return or receipt state.

Headline recommendation. Rebuild Home around balance, sticker status and the last few transactions; make "Reload" a one-tap primary action; retire or hide the two dead tabs until they work natively; replace the SOA generator with an in-app transaction list. These four moves address roughly 70% of the friction identified below and are all achievable without changing the underlying account model.

What works

  • Strong, consistent brand green; the header + card language is recognizable and calm.
  • Customer Care exposes per-tollway hotlines and a structured concern form — the right ingredients, if not the right presentation.
  • Multi-account support ("My Accounts", "+ Add") anticipates households and fleets.
  • Password fields have Show toggles; legal documents are readable in-app rather than external PDFs.
  • The Payment Summary (R4, R9) is the best-designed screen in the app: itemised amount, fee and total, one clear primary action, a quiet "Go Back."
  • Six real payment channels across banks and wallets, each with its logo — broader coverage than most local utility apps.

Observed user goals and primary journeys

Goals are inferred from what the screens make possible and from the concern categories the app itself lists in Customer Care (Incorrect Charging, Uncredited Load, Unreadable Sticker, Lost Card, Request for SOA). Those categories are the company's own record of why customers contact them — a useful proxy for top jobs-to-be-done.

J1 · "Do I have enough load?" (highest frequency)

Splash → Home → Balance → Account screen (₱128.00)

Three screens and two taps to see one number. No low-balance warning, no minimum-balance guidance, no "enough for your usual trip?" context.

J2 · "Top up before I hit the gantry" (high frequency, high urgency)

Home → Balance → Account → method → amount + provider → Payment Summary (fee shown) → bank login webview / GCash handoff → (return & receipt not captured)

Seven screens from Home to the point of paying. Reload is a secondary section on the Account screen, below Delete Account; the amount is typed before a provider is chosen and the fee appears only at the summary.

J3 · "Was that charge correct?" (medium frequency, high trust impact)

Home → SOA → 6 dropdowns → Generate → (output not captured)  |  or Menu → Customer Care → Incorrect Charging

No transaction list exists. Users must guess a date range and generate a statement to see individual tolls.

J4 · "Something's wrong — get help" (low frequency, very high impact)

Menu → Customer Care → pick hotline or Message Us (Account → Concern → Message → Send)

Functional, but numbers are hidden behind identical icons, and there is no ticket status after sending.

J5 · "Add my second car / family member's tag" (one-time)

Home → + Add → Account Number + Plate/Card Number + Alias → Add Account

Requires the 7-digit account number from the physical card or SOA; no scan, lookup, or "where do I find this?" help.

J6 · "Check traffic before I leave" (situational)

Traffic Updates tab → blank → spinner → privacy modal → web map

Reachable in principle; in practice blocked by a consent modal for a 2018 policy on every visit observed.

Assumption. Frequency and urgency rankings are inferred from the domain (prepaid toll RFID) and from the app's own concern taxonomy, not from analytics. Validate with usage data and support-ticket volumes — see Research questions.

Screen-by-screen UX audit

Screens are grouped by surface. References use the capture number (e.g. 8042) or, for the reload flow added in the second batch, R1–R10 in the order supplied: R1 Credit Card · R2 Add Credit Card · R3 Direct Debit · R4 Direct Debit summary · R5 Loading · R6–R7 BPI login · R8 E-Wallet · R9 E-Wallet summary · R10 GCash handoff.

Splash

8041

Full-bleed green, logo, determinate-looking progress bar, "Checking connection…"

  • The bar reads as determinate but the step is a network check — a user on a weak signal (2 bars in the capture) has no idea whether to wait or retry. No offline state was captured.
  • "Checking connection…" is system-centred copy; users don't need to know the step, they need to know the app is opening.

Brand-forward, no clutter, fast path into the app.

Home

8042 8049

"Welcome back, AUTOSWEEP TO GO" · My Accounts · one card labelled MAIN ACCOUNT with account no. 3631685, plate D109224, and three shortcuts (Vehicles, SOA, Balance) · "+ Add" · bottom tabs Home / Toll Calculator (greyed) / Traffic Updates / Menu.

  • No balance on Home. The screen's primary real estate is a greeting and an account number; roughly 60% of the viewport is empty.
  • The greeting shows AUTOSWEEP TO GO where a person's name is expected. Either the registered name field holds a placeholder, or the greeting is bound to the wrong field. Either way it undermines the "welcome back" intent.
  • Card hierarchy leads with the account number (green, large) over the plate. Customers recognise plates; account numbers are a back-office key.
  • "SOA" is an unexplained acronym on the most-used screen. "MAIN ACCOUNT" is a label with no contrast when there is only one account.
  • The Toll Calculator tab is rendered disabled but remains tappable; tapping it (8049) shows a native alert "Feature Disabled — Toll Calculator is currently disabled." A dead primary tab teaches users the nav can't be trusted.
  • No recent activity, no sticker status, no notifications entry point.

Account card with shortcuts is a sound pattern to build on; "+ Add" is discoverable.

Vehicle Info

8043

Account number heading · "LIST OF VEHICLES" · one card: plate D109224, tag 5154171221, Color N/A, Make & Model (blank).

  • Half the card is empty or "N/A". Rendering blank fields signals incomplete data without offering a way to complete it.
  • The card looks tappable (icon, card chrome) but no detail or action is evident. No sticker status (active / blocked / for replacement), no "report unreadable sticker" shortcut despite that being a listed support concern.
  • Navigation switches from the tab bar to a single centered "Back" pill in the footer — the tab bar disappears on every child screen, so users lose their place in the app.

Generate SOA

8044 8046 8047 8048

"Generate From" and "Generate To", each built from three dropdowns (Month / Day / Year). Defaults: From = To = today. "To" Month and Year are disabled. Dropdown-open states captured for Month, Year and Day.

  • Six dropdowns to pick a date range where a native range picker or presets ("This month", "Last 30 days", "Last billing period") would take one tap.
  • Default range is today-to-today — the least useful range possible. A user who taps Generate immediately gets an empty statement.
  • "To" Month and Year are disabled with no explanation. Users cannot tell whether the range is capped to one month by policy or by bug.
  • Open dropdown lists float over the Generate button and past the Back control (8048); the Year list shows 2021–2026 with no scroll affordance; month abbreviations are inconsistent ("Sept" in From, "Sep" in To).
  • Inputs sit inside a dashed outer border with a solid inner border — a double frame that reads as a drop-zone or a disabled state, not an input. Focus is indicated with an orange dashed ring, a colour otherwise used for the Cancel button.
  • The statement output was not captured; it is unclear whether users see it in-app, download a PDF, or receive an email. That uncertainty is itself a design problem — nothing on the form sets the expectation.

Account (Balance & Reload)

8045

CURRENT BALANCE ₱128.00 · ACCOUNT | PLATE 3631685 | D109224 · VEHICLE ALIAS Not Set · two circular actions: Update Alias, Delete Account · "Reload Your Account": Credit Cards, Direct Debit, E-Wallet · "Powered by Xendit Philippines".

  • Delete Account has identical visual weight to Update Alias and sits directly above the reload options. A destructive action has no colour, position or friction signal. (Confirmation dialog not captured — validate.)
  • Reload — the primary action for a prepaid product — is a headed section of three equal cards, not a primary button. No amount presets, no last-used method, no minimum/maximum guidance.
  • "E-Wallet — Connect a digital wallet for easy transactions" describes linking, not paying; "Direct Debit" is a banking term most consumers read as "the bank takes money automatically." Copy should describe the customer's action ("Pay with GCash / Maya", "Pay from my bank").
  • Screen title "Account" doesn't match the Home shortcut "Balance" that leads here.
  • The Xendit attribution is fine for trust; the handoff itself is audited below under R1–R10.

Balance is large and legible; the three reload options are clearly distinguished with icons.

Reload · Credit Card

R1 R2

R1: "Enter Reload Amount" field · "Your Credit Cards" list with one masked Mastercard and a red trash icon · a floating green "+" far below · "Powered by Xendit" · Back. R2 "Add Credit Card": Card Number, Expiry Month/Year dropdowns, CVV, First Name, Last Name, Email Address, Mobile Number, Save Card.

  • No visible "Pay" action on R1. The user presumably taps the card row, but nothing signals that; the only buttons are delete (red, prominent) and add. The primary path is invisible while the destructive one is loud.
  • Amount is requested before a card is chosen, with no currency prefix, presets, or min/max — and it is re-entered on every method screen if the user changes their mind (R1, R3, R8 each have their own amount field).
  • R2 collects the full PAN, expiry and CVV in what looks like a native Autosweep form with the app's dashed inputs. Whether this is a Xendit tokenisation component or the app's own form is not visible to the user — and that is the problem: there is no lock icon, no "your card details go directly to Xendit" line, no card-brand detection or number formatting, no scan-card option.
  • Email and mobile are requested again even though the account already has them.
  • Expiry uses two dropdowns instead of a single MM/YY field.

Saved-card list with brand logo and masking is the right model.

Reload · Direct Debit

R3 R4 R5 R6 R7

R3: amount field · "Choose Bank": BPI, RCBC, UnionBank cards with logos. R4 "Payment Summary": illustration · RFID Account Number, Payment Method, Bank, Reload Amount ₱200.00, Convenience Fee ₱13.00, Total ₱213.00 · "Proceed to Payment" · "Go Back". R5: full-screen "Loading…" spinner. R6–R7: an embedded BPI "Login to your account — You need to login to link with XENDIT" page with Username, Password, an authorisation notice and a red BPI "Login" button, inside the Autosweep header and Back pill.

  • Bank credentials are typed inside the Autosweep app chrome. The BPI page is a webview under a green "Direct Debit" header with no URL bar, no padlock, no way to verify the domain. This is exactly the pattern banks tell customers to refuse. Even if the page is genuinely BPI-hosted, the presentation is indistinguishable from phishing.
  • The copy says "link your BPI Online Banking account to XENDIT" — a third-party name the user met once in a footer. It reads as authorising a standing link, not paying ₱213 once.
  • The ₱13 fee appears for the first time on the summary, after amount and bank are chosen. On a ₱200 load that is 6.5%. Neither the Account screen nor the method screens mention a fee.
  • Provider descriptions vary without meaning: "Pay instantly with your BPI Account" / "Use your RCBC balance for payment" / "Pay securely via your UnionBank account." Three phrasings imply three behaviours; there is one.
  • R5's loading state occupies the whole screen with no context ("Connecting to BPI…") and the Back pill remains live mid-transaction.
  • The illustration on R4 takes a third of the viewport and pushes the numbers below the fold on smaller devices.

Summary is itemised, total is emphasised, "Go Back" is correctly de-emphasised; branded spinner (vs. the Traffic tab).

Reload · E-Wallet

R8 R9 R10

R8: amount field · "Choose E-Wallet": GCash, GrabPay, Maya. R9: Payment Summary identical to R4 with Provider "GCASH", fee ₱13.00, total ₱213.00. R10: GCash-branded panel "Securely complete the payment with your GCash app" · "Open in GCash" · "or Log in to GCash and scan this QR with the QR Scanner" · large empty white area · Autosweep Back pill.

  • The QR panel is blank. Either it failed to render or it is still loading — either way the fallback instruction points at nothing. A user on the same phone cannot scan their own screen anyway; the QR path is only useful on a second device and should say so.
  • After "Open in GCash," the return to Autosweep, the success/failure state and any receipt are not captured. Given "Uncredited Load" is a named support category, this return leg is the highest-risk unobserved step in the app.
  • Provider is rendered "GCASH" on R9 but "GCash" everywhere else.
  • Same fee (₱13) as Direct Debit with no explanation of whether it is flat or per-method.

Deep-link "Open in GCash" is the right primary path; wallet logos are recognisable.

Traffic Updates

8050 8052 8053 8054 8055

Tab opens to a blank white page → a four-dot loader in non-brand colours → an embedded SMC Tollways website with a "We have an updated Privacy Statement" modal (pre-checked consent box, "I AGREE") overlaying a traffic map with feed timestamps ("STAR: 34 minutes ago", "TPLEX: 2 minutes ago") → tapping the link navigates the whole tab to a Privacy Statement page dated 15 June 2018, complete with the website's own hamburger menu.

  • This is a webview of a desktop website, not a feature. It has its own navigation, its own typography (Ubuntu), its own consent flow, and no way back once the user follows a link.
  • A consent modal for a policy last updated in 2018 interrupts every visit observed. The checkbox is pre-ticked, which is both a dark pattern and likely non-compliant with informed-consent norms under the Data Privacy Act.
  • Three distinct loading states (blank, spinner, content) with no skeleton or message; the spinner's red/blue/purple palette is off-brand and looks like an error.
  • The underlying content — per-tollway feed freshness — is valuable and worth surfacing natively.

Menu

8051 8056

AUTOSWEEP TO GO · email · View Annoucement · Change Password · Customer Care · Legal Notice · "Autosweep App Version 4.0.0.1" · Logout.

  • Typo: "Annoucement". Label drift: "Change Password" opens "Update Password"; "Legal Notice" opens "Legal Notices".

Current / New / Retype password with Show toggles; full-width CTA.

Customer Care

8059–8065

"Need Help?" · five identical green circles (Autosweep, Skyway, SLEX, TPLEX, STAR) that open a native call sheet with a number · "Message Us" form: Account Number dropdown ("Not Applicable" / 3631685), Type of Concern dropdown (10+ items), Message textarea · orange Cancel + green Send Message.

  • Hotline numbers are hidden until tapped; users can't copy, save, or compare them. No operating hours. Two of the five buttons share the car icon and two share the bus icon, so the icons carry no meaning.
  • "Not Applicable" as the first account option is internal vocabulary; the account should be pre-selected when there's only one.
  • Concern list mixes jargon ("ADA Status", "E-Pass Balance Transfer") with plain items and is long enough to scroll under the Back control (8061, 8062). No search, no grouping, no explanation.
  • Cancel is a saturated orange block with equal weight to Send — the destructive-looking button is the one users hit by mistake.
  • No indication of what happens after Send: no reference number, no expected response time, no history of past messages.
  • Keyboard overlaps the action buttons when typing the message (8065).

Per-tollway hotlines and a categorised form are the right building blocks; call sheet confirms the number before dialling.

Legal Notices, EULA, Privacy Policy, Terms & Conditions

8066–8070

Three list items; each opens a long-form document in-app.

  • The EULA is between the user and "Intelligent E-Processes Technologies Corp. (IETC)" — a name that appears nowhere else in the app. The T&C lists seven tollway companies. Customers who think they are dealing with "Autosweep" or "San Miguel" meet an unexplained cast of entities exactly where trust matters.
  • On the Privacy Policy screen (8068) the iOS home indicator renders as a black bar over the header and over the Back label — a safe-area layout bug.
  • No table of contents, no "last updated" date in the in-app versions, no search. Fine for compliance, poor for anyone trying to find the minimum-balance or sticker-replacement clause.

Readable typography, native scrolling, no PDF handoff.

Add Account

8071

Enter Account Number (1234567) · Enter Plate/Card Number (ABC1234 or A123456) · Enter Vehicle Alias · Add Account.

  • No guidance on where the account number lives (RFID card? sticker receipt? SOA?). A first-time user with only a sticker on the windshield may be stuck.
  • No scan (card barcode / OCR), no lookup by plate + OTP, no explanation of "Plate/Card" as two alternatives.
  • Alias is requested but the existing account shows "Not Set" — inconsistent expectations between add and existing flows.

Key pain points and evidence

Thirty-two findings, each with severity, impact, recommendation and validation needs. Severity weighs frequency (how many users hit it), impact (what it costs them) and trust (whether it makes the customer doubt the product or the company).

F1

Balance is hidden from Home

Critical
Screen / flow
Home 8042 → Account 8045 (J1)
Problem
The app's most-checked value requires navigating to a secondary screen. Home shows a greeting, an account number and empty space.
User impact
Every session costs an extra tap; drivers checking at a stoplight or before a gantry get no answer at a glance. Increases the chance of an insufficient-balance stop at the toll plaza.
Recommendation
Show balance per account on the Home card, with a status colour (OK / low / critical) and a one-tap "Reload" button in the card itself.
Rationale
Prepaid products (telco load, e-wallets) universally lead with balance; users' mental model is "how much do I have," not "which account am I."
Validate
Confirm balance is available from the accounts endpoint at Home load time; confirm minimum-balance threshold per tollway to drive the status colour.
F2

Traffic Updates is a webview blocked by a consent modal

Critical
Screen / flow
Traffic Updates tab 8050 8052 8053 8054 8055 (J6)
Problem
One of four primary tabs embeds the SMC Tollways website. It loads blank → spinner → a "We have an updated Privacy Statement" modal (pre-ticked checkbox) over the map. Following the link replaces the tab with a 2018 policy page and the website's own nav.
User impact
Feature is effectively unusable on first visit; users are trapped in a website inside a tab with no back. Pre-ticked consent damages trust and may be non-compliant.
Recommendation
Short term: suppress the web consent modal inside the app (consent is already covered by the app's own Privacy Policy) and lock the webview to the map route. Long term: render a native traffic view from the same feed (per-tollway status + freshness).
Rationale
A primary tab must work on the first tap; consent should be collected once, in-app, not by an embedded site.
Validate
Whether the modal recurs after acceptance (cookie persistence in the webview); whether a JSON feed exists behind the map.
F3

Toll Calculator tab is present but disabled

Critical
Screen / flow
Home tab bar 8042 → alert 8049
Problem
A greyed primary tab remains tappable and produces "Feature Disabled — Toll Calculator is currently disabled." No reason, no ETA, no alternative.
User impact
Half of the primary navigation is dead (with F2). Users learn the nav is unreliable and stop exploring; a frequently requested capability (fare lookup) is advertised then withheld.
Recommendation
Remove the tab until the feature ships (three tabs is fine), or replace it with a static fare-matrix view that needs no live service. If it must stay, show an inline "Coming back soon" state instead of a modal.
Rationale
Primary navigation is a promise; every item must resolve to something useful.
Validate
Why the feature is disabled (backend cost? accuracy?), and how often users tap it — analytics will show demand.
F4

No transaction history; SOA generator is the only path to charges

Critical
Screen / flow
Home → SOA 8044 8046–8048 (J3)
Problem
Users cannot see individual tolls or reloads anywhere. The SOA form needs six dropdown selections, defaults to a same-day range, disables half the "To" fields without explanation, and never says what "Generate" produces.
User impact
"Was I charged correctly?" and "Did my load go through?" — the two most trust-sensitive questions — have no direct answer. This is consistent with "Incorrect Charging" and "Uncredited Load" being named support categories.
Recommendation
Add a native, reverse-chronological transaction list (tolls, reloads, adjustments) on the account screen with per-row detail (plaza, lane, time, amount, balance after). Keep SOA as an "Export statement" action with a native date-range picker and presets.
Rationale
A list users can scroll beats a form users must configure. Self-service reduces support volume on the two largest concern categories.
Validate
Transaction data latency from plazas; what the generated SOA currently looks like and how it's delivered.
F5

Reload is a secondary section, not a primary action

High
Screen / flow
Account 8045 → R1 R3 R8 (J2)
Problem
The core revenue and satisfaction action lives below alias/delete controls as three equal cards. Seven screens separate Home from the payment step. The amount is typed on each method screen (re-entered if the user switches), with no presets, currency prefix, or min/max. No saved default method; no post-payment confirmation captured.
User impact
Slower top-ups under time pressure; more abandoned reloads; "Uncredited Load" tickets when the return leg isn't confirmed in-app.
Recommendation
Primary "Reload" CTA on Home and Account → one amount step (presets ₱200 / ₱500 / ₱1,000 / custom, limits shown, fee preview) → one method step listing all six providers plus saved cards, last-used pre-selected → summary → in-app receipt with reference number and new balance.
Rationale
Standard prepaid top-up pattern; collapsing method-then-amount-then-provider into amount-then-provider removes two screens and the duplicate amount entry.
Validate
Reload funnel drop-off per method; whether Xendit returns a callback the app can show.
F6

Delete Account has the same weight as Update Alias

High
Screen / flow
Account 8045
Problem
Two identical green circles side by side; the destructive one is adjacent to the reload section users scan toward.
User impact
Accidental taps; anxiety; if no confirmation exists, catastrophic (unlinking the account from the app).
Recommendation
Move to an overflow/"Manage account" screen, style as a text-level destructive action, require confirmation that explains consequences ("This removes the account from this app. Your RFID and balance are not affected.").

F7

Announcement screen renders empty

High
Screen / flow
Menu → View Announcement 8057
Problem
White screen with no loading, empty or error state.
User impact
Users assume the app is broken; official communications (rate changes, outages, sticker recalls) go unseen.

F8

Navigation model breaks on every child screen

High
Screen / flow
All child screens: 8043 8044 8045 8057–8071
Problem
The tab bar disappears and is replaced by a lone centered "Back" pill; the header has no back affordance; no title breadcrumb indicates which tab you came from.
User impact
Users lose orientation and must back out to Home to switch context. Back is also the only control in a 44px-tall footer, wasting space.
Recommendation
Keep the tab bar persistent; put Back in the header (iOS-standard leading chevron + title); use the footer for the screen's primary action (e.g. "Reload", "Generate").
Rationale
Platform conventions reduce learning; persistent tabs preserve place.
Validate
Check Android build for parity — this may be a cross-platform framework default.

High
Screen / flow

Problem

F10

Support form: hidden numbers, jargon categories, no follow-through

High
Screen / flow
Customer Care 8059–8065 (J4)
Problem
Five identical circles hide the numbers; icons duplicate; no hours. Concern list includes "ADA Status", "E-Pass Balance Transfer", "Not Applicable". After Send there is no reference, ETA, or history.
User impact
Wrong hotline dialled; wrong category chosen; duplicate tickets because users can't tell if the first went through.

Rationale
Visible information beats hidden; closure reduces repeat contacts.
Validate

Security-aware customers abandon (banks explicitly warn against this pattern); less-aware customers are trained to accept it, which is worse. Either way, the app's most sensitive moment looks like phishing.
Recommendation
Open bank authentication in SFSafariViewController / Chrome Custom Tab (visible domain and padlock) or via the bank's app deep-link where available; precede it with an interstitial: "You'll sign in on BPI's secure page. Autosweep never sees your bank password." Disable the app Back control while the bank session is live.
Rationale
Trust at the payment step is the whole product; a verifiable domain is the minimum signal.
Validate
Whether Xendit's Direct Debit SDK supports an external-browser or app-to-app mode for each bank.

High

Problem
₱13.00 on a ₱200.00 reload (6.5%) appears for the first time after amount and provider are committed. No screen before the summary mentions a fee, and nothing explains whether it is flat, per-method, or tiered.
User impact
Surprise cost at the point of commitment; users either abandon or resent every reload. Small-value top-ups — the ones drivers make in a hurry — are hit hardest proportionally.

Recommended design solutions

Per-finding recommendations are above; this section groups them into five coherent design moves so they can be scoped as work packages rather than 32 tickets.

1. A balance-first Home

Each account card shows alias/plate → balance (large, tabular numerals) → status chip (OK / Low / Reload now) → primary "Reload" button. Below the cards: last 3 transactions and a dismissible announcement banner. Shortcuts collapse into the card (Vehicles, Statement). This resolves F1, F7 (banner), F9, F16 and gives F5 its entry point.

2. A native Activity screen replacing "Generate SOA"

Reverse-chronological list grouped by day; row = plaza/lane or "Reload via GCash", time, amount, running balance. Filter chips (Tolls · Reloads · Adjustments). Tap → detail with "Dispute this charge" that pre-fills the support form. "Export statement" in the header opens a native range picker with presets. Resolves F4, F13, and removes two support categories from the form (Request for SOA, Incorrect Charging become self-serve first).

3. A three-step Reload flow

Amount (presets + custom, limits and live fee preview "You'll pay ₱213") → Provider (one grouped list: saved cards · banks · wallets, each with its logo and fee, last used pre-selected) → Summary → provider authentication in an external secure browser or app deep-link, with an interstitial explaining what will happen → in-app receipt with reference number and new balance, plus a push notification when the load is credited. Resolves F5, F18, F27, F28, F29, F30, F31, F32 and targets the "Uncredited Load" category.

4. A conventional navigation shell

Persistent bottom tabs: Home · Activity · Traffic · Help · Menu (drop the disabled calculator; bring Help up from Menu since it is a top-5 job). Child screens use an iOS header with leading back chevron and title; footer reserved for primary actions. Resolves F3, F8, F10 (discoverability).

5. A design-system pass

Input: solid border, green focus ring, red error with inline copy. Buttons: one filled primary per screen; secondary as ghost; destructive as red text with confirm sheet. Captions ≥4.5:1. Loading = branded skeleton; every list has empty and error states; offline shows cached balance. Content: one term per concept, acronyms expanded. Resolves F6, F14, F15, F17, F20, F22–F26.

Longer-term opportunities

Activity-centred information architecture

Home · Activity · Traffic · Help · Menu, with Activity as the trust surface and Home as the action surface. Requires a transactions API with plaza-level detail and acceptable latency.

Proactive balance management

Thresholds, alerts, auto-reload, "enough for your usual route" estimates from trip history. Requires saved payment methods and a notification service.

Native traffic & advisories

Consume the feed behind the current web map; add plaza-level status and push advisories. Turns a broken tab into a daily habit.

Self-service account lifecycle

Sticker replacement requests, plate changes, mobile-number updates, vehicle details — each currently a support ticket category — as in-app flows with status tracking.

Fleet & household accounts

Shared wallets, per-vehicle limits, monthly statements, corporate installation requests — the "Corporate Account Installation" concern suggests latent demand.

Interoperability readiness

If Autosweep–Easytrip interoperability expands, the app needs a unified balance/route story. Designing Activity and Reload with multi-operator rows in mind avoids a rebuild.

Questions requiring user research or product validation

Behavioural (analytics)

  • What share of sessions end after viewing balance? How many taps precede the first reload?
  • Tap rate on the disabled Toll Calculator tab and on Traffic Updates; bounce rate from the consent modal.
  • Reload funnel per channel: method → amount → provider → summary → auth → return → credited. Where do users drop — and does abandonment spike at the fee reveal (R4/R9) or at the bank login (R6/R7)?
  • Distribution of reload amounts; how many are ≤ ₱300, where the ₱13 fee is ≥ 4%?
  • SOA generation volume and the distribution of ranges chosen.

Attitudinal (interviews, 8–12 customers across single-vehicle, multi-vehicle, fleet)

  • When and where do people check their balance? (in-car, before leaving, at the plaza?)
  • How do they currently verify a charge? Do they trust the SOA?
  • What happened the last time a sticker didn't read or a load didn't credit — what did they do, and how long did resolution take?
  • Which support categories do customers recognise, and what do they call those problems in their own words?

Product / operational

  • Why is Toll Calculator disabled, and is a static fare matrix acceptable?
  • Does a machine-readable feed exist behind the SMC traffic map?
  • What does "Delete Account" actually do — unlink from the app, or close the RFID account?
  • What does the generated SOA look like and how is it delivered?
  • Fee schedule: is ₱13 flat, per-channel, or tiered? Who sets it (Xendit, the operator)? Are there fee-free channels?
  • Is the Add Credit Card form a Xendit-hosted tokenisation component or the app's own form? (Determines PCI scope and how much can be restyled.)
  • Does Xendit's Direct Debit integration support external-browser or bank-app authentication instead of an in-app webview?
  • Is the blank QR on the GCash handoff a rendering failure or a slow load?
  • Latency from plaza transaction to backend availability; feasibility of push on credit.
  • Which profile fields can be self-edited under KYC/data-privacy policy? Is plate + OTP lookup permissible?
  • Is the "AUTOSWEEP TO GO" name a data issue or a placeholder in the tester's profile?
  • Minimum balance and load limits per tollway (needed for the status chip and reload presets).

Not observable from the screens (needs capture)

  • Login, registration, forgot password, OTP.
  • The post-payment return leg for each channel: success, failure, timeout, receipt, and the balance update on Home.
  • Credit-card payment past the saved-card list (R1) — the tap target and any 3-D Secure step.
  • Confirmation dialogs for Delete Account and Update Alias.
  • Offline / error states; Android parity.

Suggested next steps for the case study

  1. Complete the evidence set. Capture login/registration, the post-payment return and receipt for at least one bank and one wallet, the SOA output, Delete/Alias confirmations, and an airplane-mode session. Re-run this audit's open items against them.
  2. Triangulate. Pull the last 90 days of App Store / Play Store reviews and, if accessible, support-ticket volume by the app's own concern categories. Map each to a finding above; this converts a heuristic audit into an evidence-backed one.
  3. Run 5 quick usability sessions on the current build with the three top tasks (check balance, reload ₱500, find last week's charges). Time-on-task and error counts give you a baseline to beat.
  4. Define the redesign's north-star metrics: taps-to-balance (target 0), reload completion rate, share of "Incorrect Charging" and "Uncredited Load" tickets deflected, Traffic tab engagement.
  5. Design the five moves in Recommended design solutions as a connected prototype (Home → Activity → Reload → Help), not isolated screens.
  6. Test the prototype with the same tasks and participants; report before/after.
  7. Frame the write-up around the trust narrative: a prepaid toll app succeeds when the customer never has to wonder how much do I have, what was I charged, and did my money arrive. Every design decision should trace back to one of those three questions.
